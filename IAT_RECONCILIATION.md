# IAT Reconciliation — working notes

Tracking the intra-account-transfer (IAT) imbalance that stops the flow-based figures
(salary, spending, capital gain) from reconciling to the change in wealth.

**Status:** 2026 IAT imbalance is resolved — `-18,576 -> -0.02` (the 2 cents left is #4,
rounding, explicitly not worth chasing). All causes below are closed except #4 (open, trivial)
and #5/#6 (open, no 2026 dollar impact currently). A **separate, non-IAT** residual of `~$855`
remains between YoY Wealth Change and the flow cards — see
[Residual beyond IAT](#residual-beyond-iat-crypto-date-boundary-gap) below; it is not a transfer
pairing problem and is documented, not fixed, for now.
Last updated 2026-08-18 (data snapshot 2026-08-18).

## The problem

An IAT moves value between two tracked accounts, so its legs must sum to zero. They don't.
IAT legs are excluded from every flow-based figure while still moving the account balances,
so the residual is *exactly* the amount by which the flow view cannot reconcile to the
wealth view.

```
2026  YoY wealth change              173,312
      flows(incl IAT) + capgain      172,455
      IAT imbalance                  -18,576   <- the gap
```

Surfaced in the UI as a warning banner on Tab 1 (`get_iat_warning` in `tabs.py`, fed by
`TransformationManager.get_iat_imbalance`).

## How pairing works

`bkanalysis/process/iat_identification.py`, `IatIdentification.map_iat`. Two independent steps:

1. **Tagging** — a transaction becomes `Type == "IAT"` because its `FullType` is in
   `iat_full_types` (`Savings`, `Intra-Account Transfert`, `Wire In`, `Wire Out`,
   `Service Charge`, `Rent/Mortgage`, `Others`, `Tax`, `Flat Capital`, `Flat Living Cost`).
   **Nothing to do with whether a counterpart exists.**
2. **Pairing** — matches on key `(Currency, FullType, -Amount)` within +/-7 days, then writes
   `FacingAccount` on both legs. `relative_tolerance = 0.0`, so the amount must match
   **exactly**. Memo names play **no** role in pairing.

`FacingAccount` is the real diagnostic. Memo-level netting is misleading — it hides near-miss
pairs that never actually matched.

The memo pipeline that feeds categorisation (`bkanalysis/process/process.py`):

```
Memo (raw from bank)
  -> Process.__clean_memo()   <- in-code normalisation (Amazon, Chase withdrawals)
  -> MemoSimple
  -> Process.map_memo()       <- Mapping/MemoMapping.csv
  -> MemoMapped
  -> Process.map_type()       <- Mapping/TypeMapping.csv
  -> Type / FullType
```

## Scale

Most unpaired legs are securities (VMID, VUKE, WIGG, XDN0...) with totals ~1e-14 — numerically
self-cancelling and harmless. The cash legs are what matter.

## 2. Chase ATM withdrawals — FIXED IN CODE

The memo `WITHDRAWAL` covers two unrelated things:

| Group | Account | Raw memo | Legs | Net | FacingAccount |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| A — real transfers | 1A 90948 | `WITHDRAWAL: FEDERAL FUNDS ...` | 2 | -61,492.24 | First Republic |
| A — real transfers | Paragon (GBP) | `Withdrawal` | 3 | -53,783.00 | Revolut GBP |
| B — ATM cash | Chase 8162 | `WITHDRAWAL MM/DD` | 8 | -3,962.05 | **none, and none expected** |

Group B is physical cash — there is no Cash account among the 46 tracked accounts, so these
should never have been IAT. Precedent is decisive: `FullType == ATM` has **482 rows,
-46,854.11, FullMasterType Recreation, spanning 2014-2024**, with 73 `ATM*` variants in
TypeMapping.csv. ATM usage stops in 2024 — exactly when Chase took over and its memos began
landing on `TypeMapping.csv:1606  WITHDRAWAL,IAT,IAT`. A **mapping regression**, not a missing
counterparty.

Chase appends the date, so each withdrawal is a fresh memo string — a MemoMapping row per
withdrawal is unsustainable. Fixed the same way Amazon is, in code:

`Process._clean_withdrawal_memo` (`bkanalysis/process/process.py`), called from
`__clean_memo` alongside `_clean_amazon_memo`:

```python
if re.fullmatch(r"WITHDRAWAL \d{2}/\d{2}", s.strip().upper()):
    return "ATM"
```

Anchored so Group A is untouched. No CSV changes needed — `ATM` already resolves through
MemoMapping.csv:264 -> TypeMapping.csv:3 -> FullTypeMapping.csv:2 (FullType ATM / Recreation).

**Verified** against all 55 distinct raw memos containing "withdraw": exactly 8 flip, all in
Chase 8162, all previously unpaired, no paired row affected. The 27-memo
`Cash Withdrawal Non Citi ATM ...` family is deliberately left to its existing MemoMapping rows.
3 tests added in `tests/unit/test_process.py`; suite 397 passed.

Also increases Total Spending by 3,962.05 — correct, cash withdrawn is spending.

**Do NOT** change `TypeMapping.csv:1606` to `WITHDRAWAL,ATM,ATM`. That would reclassify Group A,
converting 115,275 of genuine transfers into spending and orphaning their 5 counterpart legs.

## 3. FIRSTREPUBLIC>CREDITCARD — SELF-CORRECTED

74 rows, 2023-05-24 -> 2026-06-19, net +444,455.13. Was 73 paired, 1 unpaired.

Rows sit in the credit-card accounts (Chase Credit 0165: 37 rows; Chase Credit 1458: 37 rows)
and face **Chase Bankaccount 8162** (50 rows) or **First Republic** (23 rows) — the memo is a
stale label from before the migration, never renamed on the card side. Pairing ignores memos,
so this is cosmetic.

The single unpaired leg was a **data cut-off**:

```
Chase Bankaccount 8162   last row 2026-06-18   <- checking stopped here
Chase Credit 1458        last row 2026-06-19   <- card went one day further
```

Payments post on the 20th and pair same-day every month; `-5,386.11` existed nowhere in the
dataset. **Confirmed self-corrected 2026-08-18** once `CHASE-BANKACCOUNT-8162.csv` was refreshed
past 19-Jun.

### Generalisable — and it recurred immediately

Any leg dated after the **earliest "last row" across all source files** is un-pairable by
construction. This bit again the moment the cut-off moved: `JPM Mortgage`'s payment schedule
(`Lake/mortages.json`) is **synthetic** — it generates entries up to today's date regardless of
what the real bank export covers — while `CHASE-BANKACCOUNT-8162.csv` is a real export that only
updates when re-pulled. Between the two re-pulls in this session, the synthetic schedule ran two
months ahead of the stale checking export, leaving 4 `HOME LOAN PAYMENT` legs one-sided
(`+11,837.48`, Jul 1 / Jul 15 / Aug 1 / Aug 15 2026). Resolved the same way, by re-pulling
`CHASE-BANKACCOUNT-8162.csv`. `get_iat_imbalance` should still split *un-pairable-yet* from
*genuinely broken*, or the Tab 1 banner cries wolf whenever one export is refreshed before
another — this class of cause will keep recurring otherwise.

## 4. Rounding — OPEN

219 near-miss leg pairs all-time: opposite within 0.01 but not exact, so the exact-match key
never fires.

```
HOME LOAN PAYMENT    <-> CHASE_CHECKING>MORTGAGE    41 pairs
HOME LOAN PAYMENT    <-> FIRSTREPUBLIC>MORTGAGE      4
NUTMEG FUND PURCHASE <-> NUTMEG FUND SALE          110
NUTMEG FUND SALE     <-> NUTMEG FUND PURCHASE       59
NUTMEG PENSION SALE  <-> ...                         5
```

Total residual ~0.4 — trivial in value, but 219 leg-pairs sit unpaired for want of a tolerance.
One side is amortisation at full float precision (`2959.368848`), the other rounded to cents
(`2959.37`).

**Fix:** set `IatIdentification.relative_tolerance` to ~1e-4, or round both sides before keying.

## 7. HOI - Edison "Edison Setup" dated a year early — FIXED

`HOI - Edison` (home insurance) tracks one manually-maintained CSV,
`Lake/HomeInsurance_Value.csv` — a small hand ledger, same pattern as `scripts.json`. Every prior
year the ledger's "Edison Setup" row and the matching credit-card charge are dated the **same
day**:

```
2023-08-25   HOI-Edison +7,269.35  <->  2023-08-25  Chase Credit 0165  -7,269.35   paired
2024-07-31   HOI-Edison +8,636.53  <->  2024-07-31  Chase Credit 1458  -8,636.53   paired
2025-07-16   HOI-Edison +8,440.36  <->  2025-07-16  Chase Credit 0165  -8,440.36   paired
2025-07-23   HOI-Edison +7,691.29  <->  2026-07-23  Chase Credit 0165  -7,691.29   1 year apart
```

The last row in the CSV was `23/07/2025,HOI - Edison,7691.29,Setup,Edison Setup,USD,illiquid` —
same amount as the real 2026-07-23 card charge, but dated a year early. The ±7-day pairing
window made a 1-year gap hopelessly unmatchable, leaving both legs unpaired (one hidden inside
2025's numbers, the other visible in 2026's).

**Fix (applied 2026-08-18):** corrected the date to `23/07/2026` in
`Lake/HomeInsurance_Value.csv` (writable from the container — unlike `Lake/`'s other files, this
one is not on the read-only NAS mount). Confirmed paired after re-pull.

is in that instance's memory at garbage-collection time — a stale `Process` object lingering in
a notebook session can silently re-blank a fix a newer instance just made. Happened once during
this session; re-applying the fix and confirming with `grep` after the notebook finished resolved
it.

**Fix:** filled in `Mapping/MemoMapping.csv:8307` (`SQ BROWARD SKI &AMP; WAKE BO` ->
`BROWARD SKI WAKEBOARD`) — that file is writable from the container.

## Residual beyond IAT: crypto date-boundary gap

**Not an IAT issue** — surfaced only after the causes above were closed and the 2026 IAT
imbalance dropped to ~0. A separate `~$855` gap remains between `YoY Wealth Change` and the sum
of the Tab 1 cards (`Received Salary + Capital Gain (Market) + Total Spending + Other Income`).

`callbacks.py` deliberately uses two different "start" boundaries:

```python
get_value_dates(year)  -> [Dec-31 of year-1, Dec-31 of year]   # wealth snapshot
get_flow_range(year)   -> [Jan-1 of year,    Dec-31 of year]   # flows & capital gain
```

One day apart, on purpose — reusing Dec-31 for both would double-count that day's transactions
and price move across both years (see the comments in `callbacks.py`). For assets that don't
trade on Dec-31/Jan-1 (stocks, bank balances) this is a no-op: "price on Jan-1" just falls back
to the Dec-31 close. **Crypto trades every day of the year**, so BTC-USD, ETH-USD, and the
CoinbasePro GBP cash balance each have a genuinely different price on Jan-1 than on Dec-31. That
one day of price movement lands in the wealth snapshot (which starts Dec-31) but not in Capital
Gain (which only sums price changes from Jan-1 onward).

Verified directly: CoinbasePro's Dec-31-2025 value was `14,867.76`; its Jan-1-2026 value (same
holdings, zero trades in between) was `15,681.88` — a `814.13` gap. Add Coinbase's own version of
the same gap (`40.87`) and the total is `855.00`, matching the observed residual (`854.61`,
remainder is the #4 rounding noise) almost to the cent. BTC-USD and ETH-USD each reconcile to
`0.00` inside their own Jan-1-to-today window — the whole gap is this one missing day, isolated
entirely to crypto.

**Not fixed — documented only, per decision on 2026-08-18.** Will recur every year, roughly in
proportion to how much crypto moves overnight on Dec-31/Jan-1. If revisited: make the Capital
Gain calculation specifically use the Dec-31 boundary (matching the wealth snapshot) while
leaving spend/salary/tax flows on the Jan-1 boundary (avoiding the double-count `callbacks.py`
was written to avoid).
